Cibo Vita · Manufacturing internship · Summer 2026

A cleaning cart designed
around the parts.

A cart for disassembled MULTIPOND components, designed around how the parts are loaded, held, rinsed, and drained.

The design problem

Fit was only the starting point.

The cart needed to accommodate parts with different shapes while leaving access for loading and cleaning. I had to consider how each part would sit in its holder, whether it would stay in place, and where water could drain.

I developed the cart layout and CAD, then used 3D-printed holder prototypes to refine the fit and handling.

Project stage: CAD completed and holder prototypes tested. The design was handed off for manufacture after my internship.

My contribution

From the cart layout to the holders.

I designed the cart’s layout and CAD and iterated the holders using 3D-printed prototypes. My work included arranging the components, providing access for loading and rinsing, and balancing drainage, durability, and weight.

This was part of a broader manufacturing internship in which I observed production equipment, identified recurring problems, and developed changes for review and testing.

Design decisions

Organizing the parts around the cleaning process.

The CAD brings the different component shapes into one cart. The layout uses separate support positions rather than treating every part as the same storage problem.

  1. Upper racks: repeated positions organize the smaller components across several levels.
  2. Lower positions: a different arrangement makes room for the larger component profiles.
  3. Open frame and casters: the assembly provides access to the parts and allows the cart to be moved.

Loading and retention

The holders needed to accept the parts without making loading awkward, while keeping them supported once placed in the cart.

Rinse access and drainage

Component orientation and spacing needed to leave openings accessible and allow water to drain.

Durability and weight

The design had to support repeated handling while keeping cart weight in mind.

Prototype iteration

Test the holder before committing to the cart.

I used 3D-printed holders to check the interfaces with the actual parts. Testing focused on three questions: did the part fit, did it stay in place, and was it easy to load and remove?

I revised the holder geometry based on those checks and incorporated the changes into the cart design. This let me evaluate the part interfaces before manufacturing the complete cart.

Outcome: the cart CAD and holder prototyping were completed, and the design was sent for manufacture after my internship ended.

Another improvement at Cibo Vita

Stopping a recurring scraper-cleaning interruption.

Product accumulated around a cutter scraper, requiring a cleaning stop roughly once an hour for about two minutes. I designed an extension/catcher below the blade path, selected a material and mounting approach, and supported installation and testing.

The installed change eliminated those recurring cleaning stops during testing. It was a small mechanical change with a clear result on the production equipment.
