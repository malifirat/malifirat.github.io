HydroFleet Drone-Mounted Soil Sampler

A lightweight soil-moisture sampling mechanism designed around reliable insertion, repeatability, and a strict drone payload limit.

Overview

The project addresses the need for faster, higher-resolution soil-moisture data. The team explored and physically tested multiple insertion concepts while balancing real-world constraints such as soil variability, stability, weight, and reliable insertion/retraction.

Key demonstrated target: 6-inch soil insertion with a prototype around 650 g, within a 700 g payload limit.

Design requirements

  • Insert and retract reliably in soil.
  • Stay within the drone payload capacity.
  • Resist drill-induced uplift and misalignment.
  • Support repeatable moisture sampling across different soil conditions.

Concept exploration

Early work considered lead-screw-driven drilling, spring-loaded harpoon concepts, and gravity-assisted/sliding-hammer concepts. The lead-screw/auger approach offered controllable insertion but introduced mass and stability trade-offs.

Prototype iteration

Mark 1 used a stepper-driven lead screw with a separate auger drive, enabling independent testing of insertion and rotation. Mark 2 reorganized the motor/auger layout, centralized the auger for alignment, reduced mass where possible, and added anchors plus a more robust probe holder.

Testing

Bench tests and soil-bucket tests exposed slippage, compaction, alignment, anchoring, and structural stability problems. Those failures directly informed the next geometry and mechanism changes.

My role

  • Mechanical concept development and CAD.
  • Lead screw, auger, probe, and anchor iteration.
  • Prototype assembly and physical testing.
  • Trade studies involving motors, mass, stability, and insertion force.
  • Mechanical Design Co-Lead beginning Fall 2026.

Recognition
