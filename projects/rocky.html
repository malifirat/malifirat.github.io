Rocky Self-Balancing Robot

Experimental system identification, MATLAB/Simulink modeling, and feedback-control implementation for an inverted-pendulum robot.

Full report ↗Physical robot video ↗

Project

System identification

Motor step-response data were collected with Arduino and analyzed in MATLAB to identify a first-order motor model. Pendulum free-response data were then fit to estimate the robot's natural frequency and effective pendulum length.

Initial controller

The first three-pole controller stabilized the robot's body angle after a disturbance, but wheel velocity and displacement continued to drift. On the physical robot, this caused the wheels to accelerate while trying to maintain balance.

Stationary balancing controller

The control architecture was expanded to a higher-order system with additional cart feedback. Target poles were selected, controller gains were solved in MATLAB, and the design was evaluated in Simulink before implementation.

Result

The final five-pole controller brought angle, velocity, and displacement back toward zero after disturbances.

The physical robot showed the same overall behavior as the simulation, with additional noise and less-smooth settling due to friction, motor mismatch, and sensor noise.
