Thermal-Fluid Systems Analysis · 2026

Spacecraft Re-entry Thermal Model

A simplified first-order model linking atmosphere, trajectory, aerodynamic heating, surface heat distribution, radiation, and ablative mass loss.

Full report ↗

Model

Atmosphere

A layered atmosphere model estimated temperature, pressure, and density with altitude. Density then fed the trajectory and heating calculations.

Trajectory

The spacecraft was treated as a simplified point mass with constant mass, drag coefficient, cross-sectional area, and flight-path angle. Velocity was updated as the vehicle descended through denser atmosphere.

Aerodynamic heating

The Sutton–Graves stagnation-point relation estimated peak convective heating. Because the velocity term is cubed, the model highlighted the strong sensitivity of heat flux to re-entry speed.

Heat-flux distribution

Lee's heat-transfer distribution was used to spread the stagnation heating across the curved heat-shield surface instead of treating the entire shield as uniformly heated.

Ablation

A simplified surface energy balance accounted for radiation and treated remaining heat above the radiative limit as energy available for ablation.

The simplified model predicted approximately 0.7 kg of total heat-shield mass loss.

Scope & limitations

The model intentionally simplified conduction, changing material properties, pyrolysis gases, surface recession, chemical nonequilibrium, and trajectory effects so the project could focus on a manageable first-order thermal-fluid model.
