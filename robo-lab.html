Drone soil sampler

The challenge

Collecting useful soil-moisture data across a farm requires a probe that can insert, measure, and retract reliably without exceeding a drone’s payload capacity. We explored a drone-mounted sampler through design sprints, concept evaluations, and physical prototypes.

My contribution

I led mechanical concept development and CAD design, iterated the lead-screw, auger, and probe assemblies, and built and tested physical prototypes. I also evaluated trade-offs among motor choice, mass, stability, and insertion force.

From concepts to prototypes

Early concepts included an embedded drill probe, spring-loaded harpoon mechanisms, and a gravity-assisted sliding hammer. The drill approach offered controlled insertion and stepwise measurement, with mass and stability as key trade-offs.

Mark 1 used separate motors for lead-screw travel and auger rotation, allowing the two motions to be tested independently. At approximately 610 g, it exposed stability and mass-distribution issues. Mark 2 revised the layout, centered the auger, and added anchors and a stronger probe holder.

Testing & design targets

Both versions were built and tested with bench and soil-bucket trials. Tests highlighted torque demands, probe alignment, slippage, soil compaction, and anchoring challenges.

The project’s design targets included 6-inch sampling depth, less than 5% sampling failure, and moisture error below ±5% on proper insertion. These were requirements, not claimed validated performance results.

Further work identified in the project included reliable 6-inch sampling, improved anchoring, eventual scaling to 18 inches, and field testing with growers.
